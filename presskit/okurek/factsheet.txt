SHORT DESCRIPTION
-----------------
Tend the farm, the chickens, and the grieving jars. OKUREK is a first-person
cozy pickle horror game set in the rotting mountains of Czech Switzerland.
It acts as a conscious subversion of the escapism found in typical cozy
farming games. Preserve what you couldn't save.


DESCRIPTION
-----------
In OKUREK, you arrive at the farm of a dying friend with a simple request:
take care of the cucumbers, and fix the broken bench. You plant, you water,
you pickle. The routine is meant to keep you busy while dealing with the grief.

But the land around you is sick. Set against the backdrop of real-life coal
mining and uranium leaching in North Bohemia, a geometric, pulsing rot creeps
across your plots every night. You cannot stop it, you can only endure it and
decide what to do with what is left. Your only company are the pickled
cucumbers on the kitchen shelf, who have plenty of opinions about the situation,
and a deer at the edge of the woods that speaks only in rhyme.

The game holds two decays side by side: a deeply personal loss, and an
industrial exploitation of the land. OKUREK is a conscious critique and
subversion of the cozy farming genre. Instead of offering an escapist fantasy
where hard work fixes everything, it asks what you owe a place and a person
when tending your own small garden is not enough to stop the rot.


FEATURES
--------
- A real daily farming loop - plow, plant, water, harvest cucumbers, collect
  eggs, seal pickling jars. Repetitive on purpose. Routine is grounding;
  watching it break is the horror.
- The Rot - a faceted, breathing corruption that spreads every night. Scrape
  it away and it comes back stronger. Not a puzzle. Something to endure.
- Talking pickles - the sealed jars on your shelf won't shut up. Each one has
  a different philosophy of helplessness: the ranter, the denier, the stoic,
  the complainer, the philosopher, the one who just screams.
- A deer that speaks in rhyme and knows more than it will say plainly.
- Two concurrent decays - one personal, one industrial. The valley was being
  consumed before you arrived.
- Two endings - one about preservation, one about acceptance.
- No death screen, no fail state. This is a story about inevitability, not
  skill.
- A boss fight against a car-sized corrupted cucumber. It earned it.
- PS1-era low-poly look with a custom vertex-jitter "stop-motion" shader that
  makes the whole world tremble like it's alive - except the dead things,
  which don't move at all.
- One 60-90 minute sitting. No grind, no save-scumming.


HISTORY
-------
I am currently developing OKUREK as part of my Master's studies in Game Design
at FAMU in Prague. My previous game projects have received awards and funding
from the PPF Foundation, and I wanted to explore something deeply personal
with this game.

The premise started simple enough (I just really like pickles and chickens),
but it quickly evolved into a vehicle for two heavier subjects: the death of
someone close, and the systemic poisoning of the environment under 1980s
resource extraction.

I grew up in the Karviná region, an area heavily exploited for its coal.
The feeling of pervasive helplessness in OKUREK doesn't just come from
theoretical research about North Bohemian uranium mining; it comes from a
lived experience of growing up in a mining region. Period details were
researched from archival photographs and checked with a regional specialist,
while the industrial structures in the game are an artistic interpretation
of documentation that no longer exists.

Cozy horror inspired by: Color Out of Space, Annihilation, No One Lives Under
the Lighthouse.
Plays like: What Remains of Edith Finch, Paratopic, Iron Lung, Squirrel
Stapler, Anatomy.
