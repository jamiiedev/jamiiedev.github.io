Translation & Localization Guide

O.K.U.R.E.K — a cozy-pickle-horror game set in the rotting mountains of Czech Switzerland. This page tells you what the game is, how every character talks, and what you're allowed to change.

The English text is the pivot. Everyone else (中文 / Русский / Українська / Polski) translates from the English, not the Czech — so the English has to carry the voice: the goofiness, the emoticons, the register shifts, the melancholy under the jokes.

Canonical short description

Match this tone when you localise store copy:

Tend the farm, the chickens, the grieving jars. The trees murmur while the cancer grows with every passing day. O.K.U.R.E.K: a COZY PICKLE HORROR experience set in the rotting mountains of Czech Switzerland. Preserve what you couldn't save.

Tone words: goofy · melancholic · absurd · outrageous · cozy · spooky · mind-altering.

2 · Where I'm coming from

You don't translate this section. It's context so your word choices land right.

OKUREK is a deliberate subversion of the "cozy farming sim" (think Stardew Valley). It attacks the fantasy that a person can, in the face of systemic and environmental collapse, successfully dissociate — retreat into their own routine and be safe there.

It works on two layers at once, and the player chooses how much to take in:

  • The historical / environmental layer: the systematic exploitation of the working class and of the land in northern Bohemia in the 1970s–80s — brown-coal strip mining and the chemical leaching of uranium. The system treats people and landscape as disposable consumable material. Irreversible ecological damage, fatal health consequences for locals.
  • The psychological layer: a study of a mind coping with trauma. The defense mechanisms a person uses to distance themselves from a reality they can't bear, and to externalize responsibility onto someone, anyone, else.

The central refusal. I wrote an essay ("Lze hrát chudobu?" / "Can Poverty Be Played?") about Mark Fisher's idea that games about suffering tend to become a safe emotional release valve — the player performs anti-capitalism / grief / empathy for 90 minutes, feels cleansed, and goes back to their comfortable life unchanged. WALL-E does your environmentalism for you so you can keep consuming happily. I do not want OKUREK to be that valve.

So the game deliberately cannot have a real good ending:

  • One ending: you fight the monster and "win" your farm back — but it is permanently poisoned, acidic, dead. You've preserved a corpse.
  • The other ending: I step in as the narrator, speak my heart directly to the player, and the game ends — unresolved, forever open, never finished. I refuse to give catharsis, because catharsis here would be a lie that calms you down with a story that makes sense. (The "Last Words" content — see §4.)

The game is not "the world is bad and everything sucks." It's the opposite: the world is interesting and worth looking at, and the question is what we can do to not hide from it.

3 · Setting & history

Place. A small, isolated cucumber homestead in the mountains of northern Bohemia / "Czech Switzerland" (the white sandstone cliffs). It is not one real place — it's a composite, inspired by many locations in that region. Don't try to pin it to a specific town.

Time. Late 1970s / 1980s. The in-game notes are dated 1978–79. The full story spans roughly two years, but the player experiences it as 5 consecutive days — there's a time jump between each day. (Between Day 1 and Day 2, months pass. The player just wakes up "the next morning".)

How much history do translators need? A lot of it is already written for you: the long documentary notes found in the world (uranium mining methods, the "Black Triangle", acid rain and the death of the mountain forests, worker health, resident health) are already in the CSV in full, in a formal period-bureaucratic register. Those texts are the history primer. Read them. Match their tone (§6). Real institution names are already fixed in English (District Institute of National Health, Regional National Committee, Hamr I Mine, Stráž deposit) — keep those consistent.

Key facts the world is built on

  • Uranium was extracted two ways at once: classic deep-shaft mining, and in-situ chemical leaching — pumping sulfuric and nitric acid into thousands of boreholes to dissolve uranium underground, then sucking the toxic solution back up. Locals feared the acid would reach the drinking water.
  • The "Black Triangle" (NW Bohemia / Saxony / SW Poland) was one of the most polluted places on Earth. Coal plants with no desulfurization → sulfur dioxide → acid rain → whole mountain ranges of grey dead spruce stumps.
  • Health: lung cancer from inhaled radon dust; silicosis from sandstone dust; chronic respiratory disease, chemical burns; measurably shorter life expectancy than the rest of the country; spikes in childhood asthma during winter smog inversions.

The friend in the story dies of an illness of this kind. It is never named as "cancer" in dialogue — only the marketing copy says the word.

4 · Story, day by day

Translators only need Days 0–2 + Chicken Break, but read all of it so you know where the story is going.

Spoilers — click to open the full plot

You arrive to look after a friend's cucumber farm while they're away at the doctor. Over what feels like five days (really ~2 years) the routine falls apart, the friend dies, the land is consumed by mining and the Spoil, and the game refuses to let you feel okay about it.

Day 0 — Arrival

Travel in by bus. A telephone stands by the side of the road. You pick up your camera. Quiet.

Day 1 — The routine

Read the friend's instructions (the letter). Put seeds in the ground, water them. The water stops flowing → walk to the intake and unblock it. Darkness falls and the deer start screaming (the first DearHead prophecy). The day ends when you get home.

Day 2 — The squirrels

Squirrels steal your cucumbers. You pickle whatever's left, then go out to find the stolen ones. The pickles talk (the five archetypes — see §9). It starts to rain; you can explore freely until the rain begins (soft time limit). If you go into the mine, you get your first look at the monster and some exposition. End of day.

Day 3 — The chickens (not in scope yet)

In the middle of the night the monster takes the chickens. You go looking for them. The rooster was killed. You find more clues about the mining and about your friend's death.

Day 4 — Industrialization arrives (not in scope)

The place is ruined. Huge pillars of smog on the horizon — industry has reached you. Everything is coated in dust. No chickens, nothing. You go to the final mine: exposition and bizarre visuals — rows of leaching pumps; cucumber-snails walking away with houses on their backs (migration / leaving / refugees).

Day 5 — Ending (not in scope)

Branches on how you played:

  • If you engaged with the small acts of care → the epilogue with the deer: the narrator speaks directly, "Last Words", the game ends without resolution. No boss fight.
  • If you only wanted a horror game about pickles → the boss fight against the giant pickle monster, then the "bad" ending: you win the farm back, but it is dead forever.

What counts as "small acts of care" (the not-boss path): watering / looking after the flower · taking photos with the camera · calling the phone numbers you find in your phone · burying the rooster in a grave instead of putting it in a pickle jar or the chicken nest.

"Last Words" / "Poslední slova" = the deer's closing monologue. In-fiction it's the deer; really it's me, the director, admitting I can't hand the player a clean catharsis and choosing to break that expectation on purpose. After this monologue the game permanently deletes the save / the game world — the point being that you don't change the real world by consuming a game, so the game refuses to sit there afterwards as a comfortable, finished object.

Chicken Break

A short, silly bonus. You see the world from a chicken's POV and the Amen break drum loop plays. Pure fun, no weight. Translate it loose and playful.

5 · Themes & metaphors

One sentence you must never lose sight of: it's a funny game with a depressing base — the player should feel joy in the dialogue and also learn something, without the game ever turning into "everything is awful".

The central question, asked over and over: Can we actually do anything about any of this? Can we? The answer the game lands on: we are the people in history and we are the people now — the ones either doing nothing, or doing something.

Translate these recurring ideas consistently (see §7)

ThingWhat it means
Pickling / preserving / jarringTrying to hold a rotting, collapsing world in place. The belief that we can keep our dead friends as long as we don't change. Sticking your head in the ground and going numb to the world.
the Spoil (a.k.a. the tendrils / the rot)The spread of the damage. Illness. The thing you cannot stop, only witness. See §7 for how to name it.
The deer / DearHeadThe player-character's conscience — an externalized inner voice that can't be silenced or ignored. Also, at the very end, literally the director.
The squirrels (incl. two-headed ones)The small, deniable first loss. "It's just nature." The mutation is already visible on them.
The talking picklesA mirror of things real people have said to me about these issues — the ways we avoid responsibility. One enjoys it, one is clever about it but does nothing, one is angry and looks forward to a revolt that never comes.
The farm itselfThe fantasy refuge. "A self-sufficient farm, where the world can't touch us." It doesn't hold.

Deliberate ambiguity is fine to keep — don't "clarify" things the original leaves open.

6 · Global style guide

The one rule

The game is serious. The translation is silly. Archaisms, goofy invented words, dumb little noises, exclamations from your grandma — yes, all of it. The horror and the sadness come from the situation, not from the prose being grim. If your translation reads as heavy and literary, you've missed.

Emoticons — keep them

:Ddd ^^ \:) :0 :33 etc. carry emotion and character. Keep them.

  • Only the narrator / DearHead uses :Ddd.
  • Every other character may use whatever emoticon fits them — you have freedom here.
  • Note the backslash: \:) — the \ is an escape and must stay (§8).

Swearing — there is none, keep it that way

The Czech deliberately avoids real profanity. No "fuck", no hard swears in any language. Preferred instead: clever / soft curses, minced oaths, "what in the name of…", regional grandma-swears; whatever your language's equivalent of a charming curse is.

Sourpuss says voe (a softened, slurred "vole" — like "man" / "dude" / "mate" as a filler). Render it as a casual verbal tic, not an insult.

Humor — you have full licence

You may replace a joke entirely if a literal translation dies. The only rule: the new joke must make sense for that character. Sourpuss might make a fart joke; Sweetie never would. (See each voice card.)

Register — reproduce the contrast, not the exact dialect

The Czech uses obecná čeština (colloquial, "street" Czech) for some characters and standard Czech for others — "it gives it the juice." You can't reproduce the specific dialect, but you can and should reproduce the gap: keep the casual characters casual and sloppy, keep the formal / stately ones formal and stately.

Sentence length

Short. Simple. Everyone except the deer speaks in short plain sentences. Don't smooth them into long ones.

UI / interaction lines are spoken by the player character

Lines like "Can't carry any more :/" or "Can't get in without a key" are the player character muttering, not neutral system text. Keep them in-character in every language — it's part of the charm. Don't neutralize them into "Inventory full".

7 · Terminology / do-not-translate

Cucumber vs. Pickle

  • okurka = cucumber — the fresh fruit, on the vine, the thing you harvest and carry.
  • okurek = pickle — the preserved thing in the jar, and what the talking ones are called.

Use cucumber while it's alive / fresh, pickle once it's jarred or when referring to the talking characters as a group. (A talking one being turned to mush mid-process can still call itself a "cucumber".)

The jarring vocabulary — keep it consistent

CzechEnglish (use consistently)
zavařit / zavařovatto pickle / to preserve / to can
naložitto pickle (in brine)
zakonzervovatto preserve / to seal away
sklenice / zavařovačkajar
"zavařit si" (idiom)"to get (ourselves) into a pickle" / "a fine mess" — the pun is intended, e.g. "Yay, we did it! We really got ourselves in a pickle."

The Spoil — the creeping corruption

The phenomenon-as-a-named-thing is the Spoil. In ordinary description it's still fine to say something is rotting / spoiled, or to call it rot. The vine-like shapes it grows are tendrils (Czech šlahouny). For "the Spoil" in your language, find a word for creeping spoilage / the turn (as milk turns) / going off / blight — creepy but not clinical, and never "cancer". Run your choice past JamiieDev.

Do NOT translate

  • OKUREK / O.K.U.R.E.K (the title)
  • JamiieDev (the developer's name / signature)
  • Bílé skály, Hamr I, Stráž, and other real proper nouns (keep, optionally gloss)
  • Real institution names — use the fixed English already in the CSV

Recipe names — go funny

Classic Dill Pickle, Pickled Fingers, Pickled Lung, The Loudest Jar, Mystery Pickle, Pickled Chicken, Pickled Eggs. These are jokes. Localize for the laugh, not the letter. (The pickled "remains" NPCs — Miner / Middle Finger / Lung / "Lower One" — are pun jokes that mostly don't survive translation. Just call the miner "Miner" and don't stress the rest.)

8 · Formatting & tech rules

The dialogue text contains inline tags and placeholders. If you break them, the line breaks in-game — sometimes silently.

TokenWhat it doesRule
[input]Waits for the player to click before the rest of the line appears. A dramatic beat / breath.Keep it. You may move it to the equivalent pause point in your sentence. Don't delete it.
[lspeed=0.07]Letter speed — how fast the text types out. Low = slow / deliberate.Keep it, at the start of the line (or wherever it is). Don't change the number unless a slow-mo beat clearly needs to land on a different word.
[lspeed=0.2] mid-lineA sudden slow-down for emphasis, e.g. N-O L-E-G-S.Keep the effect on the equivalent emphasized word.
\: \:) \:DddThe \ escapes the colon so Dialogic doesn't read :) as a command.Always keep the backslash before : in emoticons.
%sReplaced by an item name, e.g. Pick up %s → "Pick up Cucumber".Keep exactly one %s. Put it where the noun goes in your grammar. If your language needs the noun in a case the insertion can't provide, rephrase around it.
%dReplaced by a number, e.g. %d / %d removed.Keep both, in the right order.
{LMB} {RMB} {TAB} {C} {T}Swapped for a control-key icon in the HUD.Keep the token exactly, including braces and caps. Don't translate "LMB".
line breaks inside a cellReal newlines in multi-line notes.Keep the paragraph structure; you can re-break for your language.

Never edit the .translation files (*.cs.translation, *.en.translation …). They're compiled from the CSVs. Edit only the CSV.

CSV column order differs between files

  • translations.csv → keys, en, cs, zh_CN, zh_TW, pl, ru, uk
  • dialogic_timeline_translations.csv and dialogic_character_translations.csv → keys, cs, en, zh_CN, zh_TW, pl, ru, uk  ← cs first, then en

zh_TW, pl, ru, uk currently contain the English text as a placeholder (English is the fallback locale). Replace the placeholder in your column. Don't touch keys, cs, or en. Don't reorder rows. Keep the blank separator rows. If a cell contains a comma or quote it must be wrapped in "…" (most editors do this automatically).

9 · Character voice cards

Each card: who · name · personality · how they talk · what they talk about · what they represent · keep vs. flex · model lines.

For every talking pickle: give it a fixed grammatical gender in your language (noted per card) and keep it consistent. Pickles can address the player directly, most often while being stuffed into a jar.

DearHead — the deer / the stag's head

the conscience · genderless · deer_prophet
Who
A deer (or just a deer's head) that appears at night. The player-character's conscience — an inner voice that can't be silenced or ignored. At the very end it stops being the deer and becomes the director speaking directly.
Name
DearHead is canonical (deer + dear, and it's genderless — keep it genderless everywhere). Carry the deer/dear pun if your language lets you; if not, a name meaning roughly "the antlered one / the head in the woods" is fine. In the CSV the en/zh_TW/pl/ru/uk columns all read DearHead (placeholder); zh_CN reads 老雄鹿 ("old stag").
Personality
Prophetic, mournful, knowing, a little smug, quietly desperate. It has seen where this goes.
How they talk

TWO registers:

  1. The night prophecies (Day 1–4): rhymed verse, couplets, folk-incantation rhythm, archaic and ominous. The hardest thing in the game to translate. Do the best you can. If you can rhyme and scan while keeping the gist — perfect. If you must choose, keep the meaning and the imagery (running to the dung and straw to find a "clean" life; the tendrils that will catch you; hiding your face in your hands; tomorrow not waiting for you under a jar lid). A rhyme that forces nonsense is worse than clean free verse.
  2. The ending monologue + the bad-ending taunt: switches to plain, tired prose. This shift is on purpose — keep it. Suddenly it's just a person talking.
Verbal tics
Uses :Ddd (only this character). In the ending, casual asides land next to heavy admissions.
"Therapy speak"
In the ending the deer talks like a present-day person who's done some inner work — it names emotions plainly ("I live in unease", "I'm afraid of change, of otherness"), talks about "being true to yourself", "change", "your place in history". A 1978 Czech farmer would not talk this way. The anachronism is deliberate: it's the player's conscience / the director, so it's allowed to sound like now. Keep that contemporary, emotionally-literate vocabulary — don't age it down, don't inflate it into clinical jargon.
Represents
Conscience; the refusal of catharsis; me.
Keep / flex
Keep the two-register split, the sense of guilt and hiding from the world, the direct address to "you", the genderlessness. Flex exact rhymes and images if a swap rhymes better and stays in the same emotional key.

prophecy"You run to the dung, the straw, the mud —[input] chasing some cleaner way to live.[input] But soon the vines will catch you,[input] that wretched life of yours."→ you fled to the farm to feel clean; it won't save you.

prophecy"Under a jar's lid, no tomorrow waits for you,[input] in fleeing, you betray the human in you too."→ preserving = betraying yourself.

ending (prose)"I'm afraid of change, of being different, afraid that I can't do anything, that I won't change anything anymore… But I'm utterly terrified that we'll stay just like this."

bad-ending taunt (mock-cheerful)"Yay, we did it! We really got ourselves in a pickle. The soil's as sour as vinegar now and nothing's ever sprouting in it again, but who needs that anyway, right?"

The four pickles — shared notes

They are allegorical mirrors — each one is a way real people avoid responsibility. The whole game is a question about hiding and responsibility, and these four are the answers people have actually given me. They know, on some level, that they're doomed.

There is also a fifth pickle (cucumber_freak) who only ever makes noises — nothing to translate there; leave its lines exactly as they are in every column.

Naming the pickles — brief

The names are placeholders and not good enough — in Czech and in English. Treat them as yours to change. When you name a pickle:

  • Keep the idea, not the word. Each name should nod to (a) pickling / brine / souring / vinegar / a jar ingredient, and (b) the archetype. The Czech names lean on a pun that mostly won't carry — don't force the pun, carry the idea.
  • A name that reads as an actual name beats a bare common noun.
  • Double meanings are gold. Example: English "Relish" = a sweet pickled condiment and "to relish / take delight in" — ideal for the denier who delights in the catastrophe.
  • If nothing pickle-ish works for one of them, an archetype-based name is an acceptable fallback.
  • Tell JamiieDev what you chose so the four stay a coherent set in your language.

Sweetie

the doting denier · feminine · style: apologist · cucumber_pleaser
Who / represents
The person who overlooks the destruction and finds beauty anyway. Everything is fine. The squirrels are just hungry. The dust is like sugar on a cake. Denial dressed as gentleness and wisdom.
Name
Sladuše ← sladká = sweet. A sweet-pickle / a sweet old lady. → working name Sweetie.
Personality
Warm, folksy, grandmotherly, relentlessly positive, quietly delusional.
How they talk

Talks about

the fatalist · masculine · heavy obecná čeština · cucumber_doomer

The fatalist. "I told you so" + "that's just life, get used to it." Has given up on any repair being possible, and actively tries to pull others down to his level so his apathy feels normal.

The person whose farm it is. They leave for the doctor on Day 1 and (later) die. You never learn their relationship to the player — friend? partner? sibling? — on purpose.

The Nurse

the phone voice · formal "you" · display name "Nurse" · nurse_char

The voice on the phone when the player calls the hospital / clinic. The player can keep calling across the years.
Name

How they talk
Polite, institutional, formal "you" (§10 — always the V-form). Soft diminutives, euphemism.
The two calls

First call — kind, gentle, euphemistic. Doesn't want to alarm you.

Second call (a year+ later, if the player keeps calling) — exhausted, blunt, done being gentle.

Keep / flex
Keep the formality, the euphemism→bluntness shift between calls, the tired humanity of the second one.

The Player

the mutters · genderless · one voice · player_anyone / player_you / player_you_and_me
Who
The silent protagonist's occasional out-loud mutters. Anonymous, genderless, no stated past. Three "voices" exist to blur the line between the player and the player-character — you do not need to make them sound different from one another. Translate all three as one voice and lock it.
How they talk
Generic — no strong regional flavor — but full of silly little exclamations and old-timey interjections. This is the charm of the game; lean in.
Exclamation set
Build your own equivalents and reuse them. "Panenko skákavá" → "Good gracious" / "Goodness me" / "Oh my stars". "Šmarja" → "Oh geez" / "Jeepers" / "Heavens".

Genderless player & friend

Czech, Polish, Russian, Ukrainian all force gender on past-tense verbs and adjectives when someone talks about themselves or is addressed. Phrase around it wherever possible (nominal constructions, present tense, plurals, imperatives). If you are genuinely stuck on a line, default to feminine — but treat that as a last resort and flag the line to JamiieDev.

Line length

Match roughly the character count of the English line. Dialogue boxes and HUD prompts don't grow. One sentence stays one sentence.

Per language

  • Shipping languages: Czech, English, 简体中文 (zh_CN), 繁體中文 (zh_TW), Русский (ru), Українська (uk), Polski (pl). No German — the de column was removed.
  • Every non-English column is pre-filled with a rough machine-level draft so the game shows localized text everywhere. Overwrite it — it's a starting point, not something to polish around, and no native speaker has checked it.
  • 中文: zh_CN is a real (rough) translation. zh_TW — dialogue and character names are drafted in Traditional; the UI / notes are still the Simplified zh_CN text and need a Traditional + Taiwan-vocab pass. Keep emoticons and RERO/LELE in Latin letters.
  • ru / uk / pl: rough drafts from the English. The deer's verse was done for meaning only — no rhyme. Watch the genderless-player rule above; the drafts don't always respect it.
